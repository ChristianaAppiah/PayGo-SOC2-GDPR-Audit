# PayGo Financial Technologies | SOC 2 Readiness & GDPR Compliance Assessment

**IT Audit | Governance, Risk & Compliance (GRC) | Control Assessment | Risk Management**

## 1. Project Overview

This project involved a structured SOC 2 readiness and GDPR compliance assessment of PayGo Financial Technologies Ltd, focusing on its IT control environment, data protection practices, and regulatory compliance obligations. 

The objective was to evaluate security, availability, confidentiality and data protection practices against relevant control and regulatory requirements, identify control weaknesses, assess evidence sufficiency and develop risk-based remediation recommendations.

The assessment applied IT Audit and GRC methodologies, focusing on control design, evidence evaluation, compliance gap analysis and risk-based reporting.

## 2. Assessment Scope & Frameworks

### SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria

- **Security (CC1–CC9):** Access management, risk assessment, security monitoring, incident response and change management.
- **Availability (A1):** Backup and recovery, system resilience and operational availability.
- **Confidentiality (C1):** Protection of confidential information and related control requirements.

### General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

- Personal data inventory and processing activities.
- Lawful basis, transparency and purpose limitation.
- Records of Processing Activities (RoPA).
- Data retention and deletion governance.
- Third-party processing and accountability.
- Data subject rights and personal data security.

## 3. Audit Methodology

### Control Assessment

- Mapped relevant control requirements against PayGo's documented IT and operational environment.
- Evaluated documented control arrangements and identified potential design and governance weaknesses.
- Distinguished confirmed deficiencies from areas requiring further supporting evidence.
- Considered control-related risks affecting confidentiality, availability, regulatory compliance and business operations.

### Evidence Evaluation

- Developed an evidence register identifying documentation required to support control assessments.
- Evaluated the sufficiency of available information before concluding.
- Identified areas requiring additional verification rather than assuming undocumented controls were ineffective.

### GDPR Data Mapping

- Documented seven personal data processing activities.
- Developed preliminary data flow descriptions.
- Identified information requiring verification, including system relationships, data transfers and third-party processing responsibilities.

### Risk Assessment & Remediation

- Consolidated 13 findings and remediation priorities.
- Applied qualitative High and Medium priority ratings, considering potential risk exposure and business impact.
- Developed recommended corrective actions and supporting evidence requirements.
- Proposed phased remediation activities and closure criteria.

## 5. Key Audit Insights

### Evidence Gaps vs Confirmed Control Weaknesses

A key assessment principle was distinguishing between documented control deficiencies and situations where sufficient evidence was unavailable.

The absence of supporting documentation was not automatically treated as proof that a control had failed.

### Accurate SOC 2 Control Mapping

The assessment distinguished backup restoration and recovery requirements under Availability (A1) from security event detection and incident response requirements under Security (CC7).

### Risk-Based Prioritisation

Findings were prioritised based on potential operational, security, regulatory, and business impacts.

Where evidence was insufficient, priority reflected the urgency of verification rather than an established control failure.

### Remediation and Audit Closure

Recommendations identified corrective actions, supporting evidence, and closure considerations to help determine whether identified gaps had been adequately addressed.
